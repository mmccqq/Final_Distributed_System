Final Project: Building the scalable distributed system
The course explores a variety of topics in building internet-scale distributed systems. In assignments, you investigated both relevant literature, techonologies, and how they solve problems for stakeholders (like in MapReduce) or in distributed systems as a whole (like in Lamport's time paper). However, in the world of systems, implementation and self-evaluation are key in attaining mastery over concepts. This project is your chance to put everything together practically and show that mastery.

Task at hand
In a group of 3 to 4 people, you must find a problem to solve, design and build your own service to solve it, and show empirically through experimentation how that design scales while handling the known issues inherent in distributed systems. In the process, you will experience the following as if you were doing research in a lab (industrial or otherwise):

Investigate a problem and propose a service you will design that solves this problem, which involves scouring for relevant documentation to show the problem exists and identifying the stakeholders of this service. This problem can be super technical or close to community or home.
Design, implement, and document this service. Your design must ensure the service will scale for internet-scale use.
Evaluate the scalability of the service to the point you could present this at a conference in front of experts.
Present those findings to your peers and be open for questioning from those peers.
Document all of this into a research-level experiential document.
Constraints
Your group must be 3 to 4 people and all students must be in this section and from the Vancouver campus. This means no one from the online section can be your group mate.
Your presentation will be in person on the last day, which means you must be physically present that day. However, that also means you don't have a mock interview that week.
Deliverables
Project Proposal (in-class on 10/26)
You will propose your service in a 5 minute presentation in-class. In this presentation you will discuss the following details:

What problem are you solving with your service and why is it important to your future stakeholders?
How will you evaluate and observe the service?
How is your service a distributed system as we have discussed in this course?
How does this service and evaluation tie into the concepts from the course that we have or will cover?
The proposal itself is only graded upon presenting something that at least satisfies the above questions. The proposal is not graded.

The proposals can be done in a group or individually (hence 5 minutes). By the end of that week, you must be in a group so you can start planning, especially if you have an ambitious project.

Group Formation on Project Liftoff (due 11/23 9am)
The group must formalize your project and members with a Project Proposal and Team Charter. This document is graded on on-time delivery and how actionable the plans detailed within are. You will find the format linked on this page.

Final Project Deliverables (due 12/14 9am)
The final project deliverables are split into the group items and the solo items detailed below.

The deliverable rubric will be released with the Canvas assignment post for the final project, but in short you can expect the following to be reviewed:

Presentation of the design itself
Evaluation of the design and how it scales
How much does the self-reflection incorporate the evaluation to critique the choices made in the design
Solo reflection and contribution on the project
The solo contribution exists to ensure every individual in the group is contributing and showing their own mastery over the course of the project.

Group Items

A link to your shared repo, and can also include links to individual repos that were active during the creation of your final project. The README should introduce the project and why you built it! The idea here is to show "activity" along the way, with how the project progressed over time (commits, issues, etc., everything you have)
Writeup on project management. This is a representation of how you moved from your initial design to your final state (however you represented this is fine, show how you broke the problem down, who worked on what, problems encountered along the way etc.)
Writeup on your experiments This is a detailed representation of experiments and results in a report that can be shared as a .pdf. This should be no more than 5 pages, and should show off your technical abilities without drowning the reader in data and details! For each experiment, please provide:
The purpose of this experiment, the tradeoff you were exploring, the limitations of what you did
The results, in detail, with appropriate charts and graphs
An analysis of the results, what evidence they revealed in terms of the tradeoffs they were designed to address, what your conclusions are based on this evidence, and what limitations there are to this analysis NOTE: The code used for the experiments should also be in the repository referenced in "Code!" above.
Solo Items

A reflection no more than 5 pages on what they personally learned in this project. This is from your own individual perspective---maybe about the most challenging bug you fixed, or experiment you ran. This is not a team effort.
